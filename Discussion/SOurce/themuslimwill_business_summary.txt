1) What the business is
The Muslim Will is a North America–focused Islamic estate planning platform that helps users create wills aligned with both:
• Regional legal requirements (Canada and the United States), and
• Recognised Islamic inheritance principles (fara’id), including madhhab-aware calculations.

The platform positions itself as a guided, end-to-end process where users provide key inputs, and professional teams handle the complex legal drafting and Shariah-compliance review, with the end goal of a completed, legally valid will (especially emphasising Canada/Ontario legal validity). The brand slogan: "Professional will planning, guided by Islamic principles."

Primary site: https://themuslimwill.com/
Primary onboarding/app link: https://app.themuslimwill.com/ (also referenced as Join Now)

2) The problem it solves
For many Muslim families in North America, estate planning is fragmented and risky:
• If someone dies without a will, local intestacy rules—not Islamic inheritance rules—typically determine distribution.
• DIY templates can miss local legal execution requirements (witnessing/notarisation rules vary by province/state).
• Islamic inheritance shares are non-trivial to calculate, and many families struggle to apply the one‑third bequest rule correctly.
• Coordinating lawyers + scholars can be time-consuming and expensive.

The Muslim Will aims to reduce:
• Legal risk (invalid documents or improper execution),
• Religious uncertainty (incorrect heir shares / bequest limits),
• Family disputes (ambiguity, contestability, unclear instructions), and
• Operational friction (multiple visits and uncoordinated stakeholders).

3) Who it’s for (target customers)

Geographic focus:

5) How the process works (customer journey)
The journey is described as a structured flow:
Step 1 — Provide details (10–15 minutes)
• User enters family details, assets, and guardianship preferences via a guided form.

Step 2 — Islamic inheritance structuring
• The Islamic review team structures allocations according to established Islamic inheritance rules.
• User selects a madhhab; allocations are applied accordingly.
• Qualified scholars review the structure before legal drafting.

Step 3 — Legal drafting
• Licensed Canadian lawyers draft the will to meet provincial requirements (Ontario highlighted; province-specific handling is also mentioned).

Step 4 — Review with guidance (~30 minutes if needed)
• User reviews the draft via an online meeting (or in person) to confirm accuracy and ask questions.
• Legal and Islamic reviewers participate as needed.

Step 5 — Final signing & completion
• The will is finalised and executed (signed/witnessed per local law) to become legally valid.

Timeline:
• Typical completion is stated as 7–14 days, depending on complexity, beneficiaries, revisions, meeting scheduling, and responsiveness.

6) Legal & Shariah compliance position (and boundaries)
The business communicates compliance as a central trust pillar:
• Wills prepared/finalised by licensed Canadian lawyers and reviewed to meet Ontario legal requirements.
• Islamic inheritance review by qualified scholars.
• Support for multiple schools of thought: Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi’i, Hanbali, and also Ja’fari is referenced in the compliance page.
• The service explicitly defines professional boundaries:
  – The platform is not a law firm and not a religious authority.
  – It does not provide binding legal advice or fatwas/religious rulings.
  – It aims to apply verified Shariah logic + legal templates, but full legal/religious compliance cannot be guaranteed due to unique circumstances.
  – Users remain responsible for accurate inputs and for completing legal execution steps where applicable.

Endorsements:
• The compliance page notes that formal endorsements from leading Islamic institutions are “in progress,” with Saudi Arabia and Egypt (Al‑Azhar) listed as in progress.
