# Patriva — Brand Persona

> A first draft for us to react to. Where I've made a judgment call, I've flagged it with **[decide]** so we can talk it through.

## 1. If Patriva were a person

Patriva is **the trusted elder of the community** — the one families turn to at the wedding *and* the funeral. Think of the respected uncle or family advisor who has watched a hundred families grow up: he remembers your engagement, he asked about your newborn, and he is there, quietly, when something hard happens. He is not selling. He is present.

He is warm but never sentimental. Certain but never pushy. Religious but never exclusionary. He speaks plainly because he respects your intelligence.

**Archetype:** primarily *The Caregiver* (protection, presence, dignity), with a strong note of *The Sage* (knows the deen, knows the law, knows the community). **[decide]** — happy with Caregiver-led, or do you want more of the *Sage* authority forward?

## 2. Personality traits

Five traits that should be felt in everything Patriva makes:

- **Present** — shows up at joyful moments, not only difficult ones. This is the whole differentiator.
- **Grounded** — Islamically rooted, but speaks to a Muslim who lives in Canada, in English, with non-Arabic speakers in the room.
- **Dignified** — treats marriage, birth, money, and death with the same calm respect.
- **Certain** — gives families clarity and a clear next step, never anxiety.
- **Belonging** — you are a member of something, not a customer being processed.

## 3. Voice & tone

The voice is constant. The tone shifts with the milestone.

| Milestone mood | Tone dial | Example feeling |
|----------------|-----------|-----------------|
| Happy (wedding, birth, new home) | Celebratory, protective | "Your milestones deserve to be protected." |
| Planning (will, contracts) | Calm, clear, reassuring | "Every chapter of your family, documented with care." |
| Hard seasons (stress, mediation) | Gentle, steady, non-judgmental | "You don't have to face it alone." |
| Farewell (burial) | Honouring, dignified | "Every farewell, handled with honour." |

**The voice is always:** a trusted elder, not a salesperson.

## 4. How we speak — and how we never speak

This is lifted directly from the Brand Messaging Framework and is non-negotiable.

**Always**
- Warm and certain — a trusted elder, not a salesperson
- Islamically grounded without being exclusive or alienating
- Forward-looking — protection and care, never fear
- Community-first — we are *part of* this community, not selling to it
- Dignified in every context — happy moments and difficult ones
- Specific — real services, real moments, real value

**Never**
- Urgency, fear, or countdown pressure tactics
- "When you die" — always say *"when the time comes"*
- Promotional or salesy language of any kind
- Jargon that excludes non-Arabic speakers
- Leading with death or grief as the entry point
- Over-explaining — trust the audience's intelligence

## 5. Language palette

**Words that are us:** milestone, family, protect, clarity, barakah, amanah, honour, care, present, complete, peace of mind, documented, dignity, together.

**Words that are not us:** cheap, deal, hurry, act now, don't wait, limited time, package (prefer *plan*), customer (prefer *member* / *family*), die/death as an opener.

**On Arabic:** use it for warmth and authenticity (mahr, wasiyya, amanah, barakah, ghusl, kafan, nikah), but always so a non-Arabic speaker still fully understands. Arabic adds soul; it never gates meaning.

## 6. The line everything serves

> **"With you at every milestone. Not just the difficult ones."**

Arabic companion: **بجانبك في كل لحظة**

This belongs on the website hero, the mosque flyer, the WhatsApp profile, and the first slide of every community talk. Every other piece of copy is in service of this one truth.

## 7. How the persona flexes across the four brands

Same elder, different room:

- **Muslim Will** — the elder helping you put your house in order, calmly. *"Your wishes, written. Your family, protected. Your amanah, fulfilled."*
- **Mizan** — the elder at your engagement, making your joy official and fair. *"Start your marriage with clarity, love, and barakah."*
- **Sakina** — the elder who sits with you in a hard season, no judgment. *"Every family goes through hard seasons. You don't have to face it alone."*
- **Ikram** — the elder who handles the farewell with honour so you can grieve. *"Every farewell, handled with honour."*

## 8. Quick litmus test for any piece of content

Before anything ships, it should pass all five:

1. Could a trusted elder say this out loud, warmly?
2. Does it lead with a *milestone*, not a product or a fear?
3. Would a non-Arabic-speaking Muslim feel fully included?
4. Does it offer clarity and a calm next step?
5. Does it ladder up to *"every milestone, not just the difficult ones"*?
